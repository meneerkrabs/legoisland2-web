### Gear report (merged)



Built by `tools/lego3d/pack/chars-gear.mjs` (+ `chars-gear-fit.mjs` search, `chars-gear-env.mjs` envelope, `chars-gear-table.json` fit table), assembled at run time by `src/mods/lego3d/chars/static.ts` (new role D). Part of `node tools/lego3d/pack/build-pack.mjs --only chars` (the chars build is 40 s in total, gear about 30 s of it; deterministic, same pack hash on two runs). Gear blobs: ~150 KB of the 0.98 MB pack.

#### Summary

| kind | replaced | kept | tris original -> new (replaced meshes) |
|---|---|---|---|
| hair | 15 | 62 | 2355 -> 4003 |
| accessory | 12 | 50 | 1724 -> 3448 |
| headgear | 21 | 105 | 3642 -> 5929 |

**48 of 265 mesh contents replaced, 217 kept** (a content = one hash; the same mesh sits under several names, e.g. 21 uses of PEPP_HRhat). In the tests the pack turns 495 gear member files of the 39 archives into LDraw geometry (218 headgear, 209 hair, 68 accessory), all parse and validate, same groups / flags / GTOM / MATL, budgets met (tools/tests/lego3d-gear.mjs).

Triangle budget: every LOD mesh <= the original of THAT mesh; **the HR meshes use exactly 2x the original** (hair 110 -> 220, hats 133 -> 265, helmets 291 -> 582, tools 48 -> 96, sword 232 -> 464): at 1x the decimated envelope of a hair or hat is lumpier than the hand-made original, at 2x it carries the part's shape. 2x is what the brief allows for HR; it is used for all 34 HR replacements (CLIK_HRhat 1.89x: its draped decal counts too), not only where it was strictly needed. MR/LR: only the 14 derived LODs (10 MR + 4 LR) that keep >= 60 triangles and pass the checks are replaced (MR 1x, LR 1x); SLR / BGR (< 60 triangles) always stay the game's (a decimated part at 20-50 triangles is lumpier than the original; a mix across LODs is allowed).

#### Method

1. **Candidates**: all 964 library parts titled "Minifig ..." (no prints, stickers, obsolete, aliases), 927 after the loader; per mesh the pool is limited by kind (name -> title filter, `KINDS` in chars-gear-fit.mjs: hair -> Hair, hat/cap/crown -> hat/cap/helmet/hood, sword -> Sword|Dagger ..., none for cloth / blades / skirts / capes / bags that are cloth).
2. **Fit**: hair/headgear: 4 yaws x mirror; accessories: the 24 axis rotations x mirror; per-axis box fit of the rotated part onto the mesh bbox (scale 0.55..1.8, <= 1.7x between axes; accessories 0.4..1.9, <= 3x because the game tools are flatter than the LDraw ones). Stage 1 ranks by a distance field of the mesh (outer-surface samples), stage 2 scores the best by the symmetric mean nearest-neighbour distance of outer samples / bbox diagonal (**score**), the mean silhouette IoU over the three axis views (**iou**), and the 90th percentile of the distances (**p90**). Ranking = score + 0.08 (1 - iou). Multi-group meshes are also tried with each small group left out of the fit (that group becomes K or D).
3. **Clean shape** (the part of the work that took longest): LDraw hair / hats are hollow, cracked, non-manifold shells; edge-collapse decimation of them stalls (my first version, shell + capSmallHoles + decimate, reached the budget for only 14 of about 60 accepted fits; the rest stalled on open borders / non-manifold edges). Instead the part is **voxelised (0.02-0.04 units), the outside is flood-filled, the head (a blocker) seals the opening, the solid is blurred and its surface taken by surface nets** (chars-gear-env.mjs): a closed manifold mesh of what can be seen; faces that look at the head from within 0.09 are dropped. Then the group of every triangle = the group of the nearest original triangle (normals agreeing), group borders kept, quadric decimation (crease 45) to the budget, and assembleStatic (UV + normals from the nearest original surface of the group, so the game's cartoon shading and textures stay).
4. **Groups**: S = rebuilt from the part; D = a small (<= 16 triangles, < 30 % of the mesh) group that lies on the new surface: the original triangles are draped on it at run time (new in static.ts: nearest facing point of the rebuilt groups + 0.012 lift, adaptive subdivision where the draped triangle would float or cut the host by > 0.02; examples: the blue shield of the fire helmet CLIK_HRhat); K = kept as is (a group the part does not have: used by the search for 3 variants, none accepted in the end).
5. **LODs**: the other LODs of an item use the same part, rotation and mirror, fitted to their OWN bounding box, built at the low LDraw resolution and decimated to their own triangle count; each is measured against its original (assembled mesh vs original: silhouette IoU >= 0.78 and score <= 0.045).

#### Thresholds and how they were calibrated

Thresholds used (T in chars-gear.mjs): **score <= 0.037, iou >= 0.84, p90 <= 0.09** for hair / headgear, **score <= 0.05, iou >= 0.50, p90 <= 0.12** for accessories (a 0.2 thick blade loses IoU to a 0.02 misalignment, I looked at each one instead); then the assembled mesh must keep iou >= 0.80 / score <= 0.045 (accessories 0.45 / 0.07) against the original. Per-item overrides (table chars-gear-table.json): `prefer` (part list chosen by eye, the pose is still measured), `relax` (LARA iou 0.82) and `deny` (judgement after looking: listed below with the reason).

#### Weak spots (honest list)

* **The replaced hats / hair are lumpier than the game's hand-made low-poly meshes.** The envelope is smooth, the decimator at 2x leaves irregular facets and a slightly ragged lower rim (where the faces on the head were cut away). In the engine the shading uses the original's normals (cartoon look kept), which hides most of it, in my flat-shaded renders it shows. That is why plain caps (INFO, NICK, BRIK / SNAP) stay original. Convincing: construction helmet (BILL, CLIK with its draped shield), castle / wide-brim helmets (BSD1, LSD, LAR), HOGG helmet, JACK / RICK / PBAK / SCUL / JIMS hair (3901), ponytails (LICK, VALS, LQUN, TRI3), long hair (LARA), JOHN's hat. Marginal: NANC (the game's cowboy hat has a dent, the library part is a campaign hat), DRKK / PEPJ helmets (chunkier than the original, the DRKK slit in front is wider).
* **Accessories are only roughly the same object**: sword family (SWORD / CEDsword / Claymore -> 2674b greatsword with its hilt colours; the game sword is a plain bar), axe (95330, blue head follows), dagger (88288), hammer, screwdriver, drill, pistol (flintlock), rifle (musket), tankard (mug), chalice (goblet). The IoU against the game's thick toy shapes is only 0.5-0.7 (thin parts), I accepted these after looking: silhouette and colours of the groups match, detail is LDraw. Not replaced: shields (no part matches the game's shape / orientation), lances, broom, bow, quiver, bags, torch (the library torch is 2x smaller), spanner, wrench (poor match).
* **Spiky hair, hair + goggles, crowns with a veil, mushroom hats, dragon helmets, visors (DRKK / PEPJ / HOGG / space), cloth (capes, skirts, turban, fez, tutu), rotor blades** have no library counterpart and stay the game's.
* Thin strands at the nape of some hair leave the odd small gap when the part is thin there (LARD: denied for that; others checked from 3 sides).
* Far LODs (SLR / BGR and every LOD under 60 triangles) stay the game's, so a replaced HR changes to the original mesh at a distance (similar silhouette, IoU 0.8-0.9, no pop larger than the original's own difference).
* The envelope is cut with the wearer's head (read from the player's archive at BUILD time, only to decide which faces to drop; the pack holds LDraw geometry only). For the heads that sit far from the hat in the inventory (NANC etc.) no blocker is used.
* Not used: the K role (a group the part lacks, e.g. a plume) exists in the pipeline but no accepted mesh uses it; sub-colours of LDraw parts (c01 assemblies) are not used (colours come from the game's groups by nearest-triangle assignment).
